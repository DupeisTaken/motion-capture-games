A runner controlled by body movement

The scaffold now includes an on-screen runner: move left, center or right to choose a lane, jump over low obstacles, and squat to duck overhead obstacles.

Temple Run / Subway Surfers are gameplay references. This plan describes an original module in the local motion platform, with recognition and tuning still to validate.

A rendered world joins the game catalog

Before

Three planned camera-overlay games: whack-a-mole, fruit slicing and dodgeball. The scope implied every game displayed live mirrored video and could grow to three players.

After

A fourth planned game uses a rendered runner world. It admits exactly one player, with camera feedback available for setup and practice. Existing game order and milestone IDs remain intact.

The new runner design brief owns calibration, lane and vertical controls, uncertainty and recovery. Product scope, architecture, component ownership notes, testing and the roadmap now point to that behavior.

There is no runnable game or before/after gameplay UI. These are documentation changes; the embedded previews below show the report itself.

Five movements, one controlling slot

MovementGame responseInitial policy
LeftSelect left laneHold selects a lane; no repeated relative shifts.
CenterSelect center laneExplicit return from either side.
RightSelect right laneParticipant-relative direction, consistent with practice feedback.
JumpClear low obstaclesOne event per validated takeoff/landing cycle.
SquatDuck overhead obstaclesHeld posture; release after a valid return to standing.

Body-size and play-area calibration precede hazards. Lane hysteresis, jump rearming, duck timing, transition collisions and obstacle difficulty require trials. No physical running in place or forward travel is required.

Unusable tracking freezes the game timeline. Pause travel, obstacles, collisions, distance and scoring together. Recovery verifies the joined slot, clears stale actions and counts down without fast-forwarding hazards.

Existing platform boundaries still apply

  • Vision: canonical observations, source timing and identity quality.
  • Motion: model-independent position and vertical/posture evidence with validity.
  • Session: one-player admission, slot binding, practice, pause and recovery.
  • Game: lane/action interpretation, runner state, obstacles, collisions and scoring.
  • Application: rendered-world composition and camera setup feedback.

Perspective graphics do not establish calibrated 3D input. Games still cannot open cameras or import inference libraries. Spectators never gain control merely by appearing in view.

M6 depends on the relevant M1 diagnostic gates and M2 session/replay foundation. Its ID preserves the existing roadmap; it does not require waiting for all other games or final packaging.
