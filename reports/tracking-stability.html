Keeping track IDs with the same person

Small box disturbances could push a motion prediction onto a neighboring person. The tracker now limits the influence of short noisy samples and checks predictions against the last observed positions before continuing an identity.

The evidence below uses generated boxes and named joints. It reproduces a software identity-switch failure and verifies the focused fix. It does not measure physical recognition accuracy or certify camera performance.

Why the identity switched

The failure

The former velocity estimate gave every processed observation a fixed 50% update weight. A brief 0.03-image-unit displacement over 16 ms could therefore create a large velocity estimate. After a slower frame, extrapolation could favor a neighbor.

The exact assignment solver and its ambiguity margins could still consider that prediction-driven match clear: optimizing a cost matrix does not establish physical identity.

The resulting behavior

Small disturbances retain the established IDs in the focused replay. When predicted and observed-position assignments support competing people, the tracker returns identity: ambiguous with trackId: null for the affected observations.

Current skeletons remain visible and counted. Implicated old IDs are retired; later separation starts new tentative IDs rather than guessing recovery.

Use source elapsed time

Velocity updates use α = 1 − exp(−Δt / 0.15 s). The time constant is provisional. The existing velocity bound and 0.15 s prediction horizon remain in place.

Check independent evidence

Compute assignment from predicted boxes and separately from last-observed boxes, including near-optimal competing observed assignments. A conflicting feasible association causes abstention.

Keep recovery explicit

Retire implicated identities and preserve existing overlap, tie, epoch and missing-input rules. An isolated predicted match remains usable when observed geometry supplies no competing association.

The tradeoff: abrupt approaches and direction reversals can interrupt continuity and require a new tentative ID. This conservative loss is preferable to carrying an established label onto another person. The change affects association; published joint positions still come from the current detection.

Assignment remains bounded to six current detections and twelve retained tracks. No inference model, camera owner, frame queue, observation schema, session enrollment, scoring rule or viewer control was changed.

What remains open

  • Physical identity: indistinguishable people or an unobserved replacement can still produce the same geometry. This fix cannot prove who a person is.
  • Provider capacity: truncation and ranking changes can change which detections reach the tracker. No capacity is reserved for an incumbent.
  • Player ownership: the diagnostic viewer has no enrolled-player lock or score ownership. Session joining and explicit recovery remain separate work.
  • Installation evidence: real one-/three-person disturbance, crossing and occlusion trials, detector behavior, recovery duration and full-pipeline latency remain M1 gates. The 0.15 s tuning needs measured installation validation.
